Data Processing Agreement

Version 1.0 · Effective 26 October 2026 · Between the Shop (controller) and the Drink Quick Cal publisher (processor), Kumba, Cameroon.

What this is. A Shop uses Drink Quick Cal to record its own sales, stock, credit and cash-up. The personal data of the Shop’s own customers and staff is the Shop’s data — the Shop decides what is collected and why. We hold and process it only on the Shop’s instructions. This agreement sets out how. It supplements the Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy; if there is a conflict about personal data, this agreement governs. It applies to every Shop automatically, and a signed copy is available on request at the address in section 12.

1. Parties and roles

  • The Shop — the company using the Service (a bar, restaurant, shop or wholesaler). It is the controller of the personal data it enters and of the accounts it creates for its staff.
  • We — the Drink Quick Cal publisher, Kumba, Cameroon. We are the processor of that data, and the controller only of our own records (subscription, billing, support), which the Privacy Policy covers.

2. Subject matter and duration

  • Subject matter: providing the Service — recording sales, managing stock, following customer credit, printing receipts and closing shifts.
  • Duration: from the creation of a Shop account until the data is deleted under section 10.
  • Nature and purpose: storage, retrieval, calculation, transmission to the Shop’s own devices, and the disclosures listed in Annex C — no other purpose.
  • Details of the processing are in Annex A, the measures protecting it in Annex B, and the approved sub-processors in Annex C.

3. Our obligations

  • Documented instructions only. We process the Shop’s personal data only to provide the Service as the Shop configures and uses it. We do not sell it, we do not use it for advertising, and we do not use it to train any model.
  • Confidentiality. Access is limited to personnel who need it to operate and support the Service, bound by confidentiality, and the sensitive actions they take in an account are logged.
  • Security. We apply the measures in Annex B and do not weaken them during the term.
  • Assistance. We help the Shop answer requests from its own customers and staff (section 9), meet its security duties, and notify a personal data breach (section 8).
  • Records. We keep a record of processing activities, and the Service itself writes the audit trail for sensitive actions (the audit log, refunds, void and cancellation traces).
  • No independent decisions. If we believe an instruction breaks the law, we tell the Shop and may suspend that instruction rather than act on it.

4. The Shop’s obligations

  • Lawful basis and notices. The Shop is responsible for having a lawful basis to record its customers’ and staff data, and for the notices it owes them — including telling them that credit, contact details and shift records are kept in this Service.
  • Accuracy and limits. Enter only what the business needs. Do not use the free-text fields (notes, reasons) to record health, religion, political opinions or other sensitive categories.
  • Access control. Give each person their own account, use the roles provided, remove accounts when someone leaves, and keep the manager approval flows switched on for sensitive actions.
  • Instructions. The Shop’s configuration and use of the Service are its instructions to us.

5. Sub-processors

We use the providers in Annex C to run the Service. Each is bound by an agreement that puts the same data protection duties on them. We will tell the Shop before adding or replacing a sub-processor for data of this kind (in the app, or by email to the address on the account) and the Shop may object; if the Shop objects and no alternative is available, it may stop using the Service and section 10 applies.

6. International transfers

Some providers in Annex C store data outside Cameroon (typically in the European Union or the United States). Where data leaves Cameroon we rely on the provider’s own contractual safeguards (standard contractual clauses or an equivalent mechanism) and on the technical measures in Annex B. The countries involved and the safeguards are listed in Annex C, and we will confirm the current list on request.

7. Security of processing

Annex B lists the measures in place. In summary: the app never holds a database credential — it talks only to our backend, which authenticates every request; the database itself is closed to the app’s public key; passwords are stored only as bcrypt hashes; sessions are server-side and can be terminated remotely; sensitive actions (voids, refunds, credit over limits, price changes, plan changes) need a manager and leave a trace; and each company’s cached data is wiped from a shared device when a user signs out.

8. Personal data breach

  • We notify the Shop without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach affecting the Shop’s personal data, to the contact address on the account.
  • The notice says what happened, which data and which Shops are affected, what we have done to contain it, and what the Shop should do. Where we cannot give everything at once, we give it in phases rather than wait.
  • We keep a record of every breach, its effects and the action taken.
  • Where a breach is likely to result in a high risk to the Shop’s customers or staff, we help the Shop work out what it must tell them.

9. Requests from data subjects

Requests from the Shop’s customers or staff (access, correction, deletion, objection) are answered by the Shop, because it is the controller. If a request reaches us directly, we pass it on promptly without answering it ourselves, unless the Shop asks us to. The Service provides what the Shop needs to answer: the customer ledger and its statement, the staff list and roles, the movement and audit trails, and the export tools (PDF, Excel, CSV). We assist with the technical work on request.

10. Deletion and return at the end

  • When a Shop stops using the Service, or asks for deletion, we delete or irreversibly anonymise its personal data within 90 days, except what accounting, tax or other legal rules require us to keep.
  • Before deletion the Shop can export its data itself at any time from the app.
  • Deletion covers the live database and the backups that rotate out within the same period.

11. Audit and information

We answer reasonable written questions about how this agreement is met, and provide the current Annex B and Annex C on request. A Shop may ask for a copy of the sub-processors’ data protection commitments. On-site audits are by agreement, at the Shop’s cost, with reasonable notice and without access to another Shop’s data. We tell the Shop if we are asked by an authority to disclose its data, unless the law forbids us to.

12. Liability, governing law and contact

  • Liability under this agreement is subject to the limits in the Terms of Service (section 14), except where the law does not allow a limit.
  • This agreement is governed by the laws of Cameroon, and disputes follow the Terms (amicable notice first, then the courts of Cameroon competent for the publisher’s registered office in Kumba).
  • Contact for anything in this agreement, including a signed copy or a sub-processor objection: the support address published in the app, or the address on the Shop’s account.

Annex B — Technical and organisational measures

  • Access control. The app holds no database credential: it calls our backend, which authenticates every request and resolves the user and the company from the session — never from what the client sends. Roles are owner, manager, administrator and staff.
  • Manager approval and traces. Voids, refunds, credit above a customer’s limit, price edits, staff changes and plan changes require a manager and are written to the audit log, with the person, the moment and the reason.
  • Passwords. Stored only as bcrypt hashes, never in clear and never logged. Older rows that were not hashed are upgraded the first time that user signs in successfully.
  • Sessions. Server-side, with an expiry, a device label, remote termination, and immediate invalidation on logout, blocking, or a device change approved by a manager.
  • Database isolation. Row Level Security is enabled on every table with no permissive policies, and the client roles have no table grants, so the key shipped inside the app can read or write nothing directly. Every row carries its company, and every query is scoped to the session’s company.
  • Shared devices. Signing out wipes the previous company’s cached data from the device, and the app can be locked by PIN or fingerprint.
  • Transport. HTTPS/TLS between the app and the backend, and between the backend and the database, with optional strict certificate verification.
  • Backups. Provider-managed, encrypted, with rotation; deletion propagates as backups rotate out (section 10).
  • Change management. Changes are reviewed and covered by an automated test suite before deployment, and the schema is versioned in the repository.
  • People. Access is limited to those who need it, bound by confidentiality, and the sensitive actions taken in a Shop’s account are logged.
